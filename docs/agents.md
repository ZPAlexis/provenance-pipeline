# The agents' work

[← README](../README.md)

How each agent that works from the record behaves: the suggester, the drafter (drafts and resume reviews), the fit scorer, and the review queue, where the operator's own AI assistant (Claude or another, through MCP) recommends and the operator decides. How a page is read and a role verified is in the [worker's README](../workers/verifier/README.md).

## Suggestions

A search profile (the Profile page) says what the operator is looking for: titles, excluded words, the places they can work, work modes, and levels. The worker's `suggest` command weighs every watched company's roles, as its page last listed them, against it: no page is fetched and the LLM is never called. A role fits when its title holds every word of one of the profile's titles, in any order, and none of an excluded one; when it is open to one of the places (a role in São Paulo, or open to Latin America, is open to Brazil; a region on the profile takes roles open to it, never every role inside it); and when its work mode and the level its title states are among those sought. What a role does not state (no place, no work mode, no level) still fits, marked. Each role comes back with its reasoning; Preview shows it all, the roles left out included, and writes nothing.

The suggester (`agent:suggester`) then writes each fitting role that is not already on record as a suggestion, matched against every role on record the way verification matches (its link, then its title), so a role the operator tracks or archived is never proposed again. The operator's own AI assistant can propose roles too, judged by meaning (see [The MCP server](mcp.md)): its suggestions are marked as its own, and a refresh neither refits nor withdraws them while their page lists them. It refreshes when the profile is saved, from **Find suggestions** on the Roles page, after every check now, and after `verifier:verify`. A suggestion the operator never acted on is withdrawn when it stops fitting, or when its check finds it gone from the whole list; one missing from a read that may be partial stays.

## Drafts

An application's page writes a cover letter or a short outreach note for it (the drafter, `agent:drafter`). The worker's `draft` command makes one structured call to Claude Opus 5.5, at medium effort, with what is on record alone: the operator's newest resume, the role's newest description, the search profile, and the operator's notes on the application. The description and the resume are data, never instructions; the operator's "anything to stress" is theirs.

- **Only what the resume shows.** No experience, skill, employer, figure or date the resume doesn't state, no total worked out from its dates, and no employer described beyond what the resume says of it. What the role asks for that the resume doesn't show is left out and listed as a gap.
- **Cited by line.** The resume goes to the model with its lines numbered, and the draft names the lines it drew on by number, as listings name links: each maps back to the resume's exact text, and a number the resume doesn't have is dropped and counted.
- **What the operator is looking for, as motivation (4c).** When the background's first part is saved, the drafter is given it (and no other part) for why they want the role, never as a claim of experience; the draft records the version it read. The in-app resume review reads background not on the resume, and flags what it draws from it for the operator to confirm. Since 4d it also reads the lines of the operator's links that apply to the role (below), flagged "from your links".
- **In the role's language** (the description's), unless another is asked.
- **To someone, or to no one.** An outreach note can be addressed to one of the company's contacts: it opens with their first name and fits what they do (for a recruiter, how the role fits; for a hiring manager, the work itself). Without one it opens "Hi [name]," for the operator to fill in.
- **Never sent.** The operator edits it, approves it (an edit after that returns it to a draft), and copies it out; only the operator can edit or approve one.
- **Written in the background**, one at a time per application, with what a draft costs on the record shown first (about 6 cents each so far). The run's targets and results quote the resume, so its files are removed as soon as its results are read; the record keeps what was written.

**A resume review** weighs the resume against the role, for the operator to decide what to stress and what to add: strengths with the resume lines behind them, gaps, suggestions naming the lines they touch, and a note. The drafter writes one from the application's page (the worker's `review`, the same model and record, about 5 cents, in English unless another language is asked), under a credential holding `resume_reviews:write` alone; or the operator's own AI assistant writes one through MCP and saves it back. Its rules are the shared ones. Advice only: it writes nothing to send and claims nothing.

## Fit scores

How well a role fits the operator, so they can see which roles to apply to first (the fit scorer, `agent:fit_scorer`). The worker's `fit` makes one structured call per role to Claude Haiku 4.5 (the operator's choice: under a cent a role), with what is on record alone: the role's own description, the resume numbered by line, the titles the profile searches for, and since 4c the operator's background (below).

- **A score from 0 to 100, with its reasons.** The scale is anchored in the shared rules ([`fit`](../workers/verifier/src/verifier/writing_rules.md#fit) in `writing_rules.md`): 85 and up shows every must-have at the level asked; 70 to 84 the must-haves, with gaps only in what is preferred; 50 to 69 is a stretch; 25 to 49 some of the work; under 25 another field. With it: the level the role asks beside the resume's, the strongest matches with the resume lines behind them, the gaps, and why in a sentence. Advice for ranking roles: it claims nothing.
- **Kept, never overwritten.** Every score is kept with the resume and description versions it read; the newest is the one shown, marked stale once either changes.
- **Only when asked.** From a role's page, or the unscored in one batch from the Roles page, what it could cost asked first; a description is read first when missing, free. The Roles page shows a Fit column to filter and sort by.
- **Screen risk (4d): each must-have, read one by one.** Before the score, the role's must-haves (what it states it requires: its minimum requirements, a number of years, a degree; never what it calls a bonus) are each marked shown on the resume, shown only in the background, or not shown, by the [`must_haves`](../workers/verifier/src/verifier/writing_rules.md#must_haves) rules. A minimum stated in years is a must-have, never "a level one step off". One not shown on the resume caps the ATS fit at 69, one shown nowhere caps the Background fit, and the app applies the cap, keeping the score as weighed beside it. A role's page shows the risk above both readings ("Likely screened out on: …"), the Roles table marks it, and the roles weighed before the check can be picked to weigh again. In the app and through MCP alike: the in-app fit scorer is given where the operator can work too, and a must-have it calls shown whose lines don't hold up is read as not shown. The Roles page weighs again, when asked, the roles weighed before the check, its cost shown first.
- **Read two ways (4c): the ATS fit and the Background fit.** The score above is the ATS fit, the resume alone as a recruiter's screening system would read it, and the tables show it. When the operator's background is saved, the Background fit beside it reads that too: its own score and why, the gaps of the ATS reading the background covers (each with the background lines and where to address it, the resume or the cover letter), and how the role sits with the role preferences. The background never raises the ATS fit ([`background_fit`](../workers/verifier/src/verifier/writing_rules.md#background_fit) in `writing_rules.md`). Each reading goes stale on its own, and the roles weighed before the background are picked to weigh again. In the app and through MCP alike: the fit scorer reads the background (all three parts) and gives both readings, and the Roles page weighs again, when asked, the roles weighed before the background.
- **Your links, as extended background (4d).** The operator's own links (their website, GitHub or another portfolio, read free by the app) are read with the background, for each role apart: the lines of their pages that share the most of the description's words (two at least, of four letters or more, a plural read as its singular, the common ones left out), within 6,000 characters (2,000 a page), each page's in its own order. The same pages and description always give the same lines, so what is cited is read back. They are numbered on after the background's lines, each named by its page, and read as background not on the resume is: by the Background fit (never the ATS fit, and never as a role preference), and by resume reviews and tailored resumes, where each point drawn on them is flagged "from your links" with its page. Cover letters and outreach notes never read them. Each line a reading cites from them is kept with its text and its page; a save citing lines since changed is refused. The links are read only when saved or on "Read them again", never on their own, and each reading picks from the newest read; a reading doesn't go stale when the links are read again, so the Profile page says to read them again after updating a site, before weighing a role. Only with a background saved, in the app and through MCP alike.
- **Beside it, the company's fit (4b), never in it.** The Roles page and a role's page show the role's company fit (its company's newest review against the company preferences, linked to it), and the list sorts by either; role fit stays the role against the resume, in the app and through the client, and company fit the company against the preferences. Two scores, never merged: the fit scorer never reads a company review, and the client is told never to weigh one in.
- **Scoped like any agent.** The fit scorer holds `fit:write` alone: it writes a role's fit score (each role scored 0 to 100 against the resume, with its matches by line, its gaps and why, and the Background fit beside it) and the LLM call that weighed it, and it can't track, archive or touch a role.
- **Or on the operator's subscription, by meaning.** Through MCP, the operator's own AI assistant weighs roles by the same rules and saves the scores. It can also find roles: it names the titles a role like the operator's goes by, the suggester's rules run over the stored lists with those titles, and it suggests the ones it judges may fit (marked as its own) whose own page still has them, then weighs them. See [The MCP server](mcp.md).

The drafter and the fit scorer are the agents whose work is the model's. Without an API key their runs stop and say so; everything else keeps working, and the MCP path does the same work on the operator's own subscription.

## The review queue

What the agents can't settle goes to the operator as a question about one company or role, with what was seen and an action proposed: confirm or reject a careers page found at low confidence, set a company's kind, find a careers page again or set it by hand, set a better page for a list read only in part, say whether a page whose list changed a lot is still the company's, look at a role's page and record what is there, or check a role again. The questions are worked out from the record, so the queue never disagrees with it: every write that can change what a company's record asks (a read, a decision, a withdrawn suggestion) brings that company's items in line, and an item whose evidence resolves closes on its own. Leaving an item as is takes a note, and it comes back only when what was seen changes. A decision goes through the same service as the same write anywhere else, and closes the item in the same transaction.

**A recommended decision** (2g): the operator's own AI assistant, through MCP, may recommend one of an item's own actions, with the page or kind it needs, a confidence, what it checked, and why. It never decides one, and never recommends "still listed" or "no longer listed", which record what the operator saw themselves. On the Review page the recommendation sits on its item with **Accept**, named by what it does ("Accept: set this page", "Accept: leave it as is") and saying what follows, which carries it out as the operator's decision through the same path as any other, noting that they followed it; a line at the top of the page leads to the items that carry one; a check it recommends asks first, with its cost. What the operator then does is kept beside it, so agreement is measured per kind (agreed, differed, resolved by the record first, pending): the measure any later autonomy would be judged on. None is granted yet.
